## What Writ was trying to do

PR #43 had established one exact decision-case handoff. This transition asked whether Writ could
preserve several separately authored analyses that shared exact evidence without collapsing them
into one model, retain their different modelling premises, process explicit source, assumption,
mapping, or context revisions, and identify which prior uses required reassessment.

The intended handoff also needed to preserve existing candidate evidence and let a recipient check
it freshly. Reassessment could not silently regenerate a result, approve a changed basis, infer
empirical truth, or confer authority to act.

## What existed at this checkpoint

The merged shared-analysis lifecycle imported complete native decision cases while keeping local
identities scoped to their originating bundles. It recognized a shared source only from exact source,
document-version, and byte identities; compared exact mathematical subjects, assumptions, mappings,
intended uses, and units; and retained explicit model disagreement.

Callers supplied revision events. A deterministic, rebuildable lineage index reported direct and
downstream effects while retaining complete, partial, and unknown dependency coverage as different
states. Reassessment was bound to the exact prior and successor basis. Original and successor
execution/candidate bytes remained preserved, and recipient replay reconstructed impacts and
freshly checked each stored candidate rather than calling the producer for a replacement.

This was a bounded lifecycle for the existing mathematical profile, not generic workflow
infrastructure or an autonomous decision system.

## Major decisions

- ADR 0028 had status **Proposed**. It proposed the portable shared-analysis revision boundary;
  merging its implementation did not automatically accept it.
- ADR 0028 kept analyses separate from corpus and native knowledge records. Shared evidence did not
  establish a shared model, statistical independence, empirical truth, or agreement between
  analysts. Exact basis determined reassessment, replay supplied checker assurance only, and human
  authority remained external.
- ADR 0026 remained **Proposed**. Its exact decision-case and pinned-checker boundary supplied the
  execution and fresh-checking foundation reused here.
- ADR 0025 remained **Proposed in REVIEW-ARTIFACT-BINDING-001; generic implementation experiment
  awaiting review**. Its review-byte work did not authenticate the shared-analysis declarations.
- ADR 0027 remained **Proposed**. The release added no service or replacement database.

## What worked

- Separately authored analyses retained bundle-local identity even when their local labels collided.
- Exact shared source bytes could be inspected without asserting model equality, and equal labels
  did not hide different modelling choices.
- Explicit source and assumption revisions separated preserved old mathematics from changed
  applicability or a changed successor subject.
- Complete, partial, and unknown dependency inventories retained different consequences for claims
  that an analysis was unaffected.
- Applicability declarations were derived from the selected exact basis rather than caller-invented
  summary authority.
- Recipient replay freshly checked preserved candidate bytes. A check-only integration refused any
  producer solve call while replaying both original and successor candidates.
- A changed basis was never silently treated as applicable, reviewed, or approved.

## What did not survive

The larger parallel lifecycle from the reuse-oriented candidate was rejected; only its small,
deterministic lineage mechanics were adapted. Caller-invented mapping and context digest fields were
removed in favor of a mechanically derived basis. Fixture-name identity shortcuts, cross-statement
routes treated as alternatives, substantive changes relabelled as metadata, and optimistic stored
success booleans all failed review and were replaced with typed, scoped, fail-closed behavior.

Replay did not accept unverified stored success as fresh assurance and did not silently recompute a
candidate. Titles and dates did not select source versions. Dependency edges did not imply empirical
truth, causality, or independence, and one analysis's output did not become an implicit premise of
another.

## What survived

Writ retained exact mathematical subjects and dependency identities, separate modelling
assumptions, immutable prior results, explicit revision and reassessment, fresh candidate checking,
and basis-sensitive reuse. Mathematical checking, empirical applicability, human disposition, and
authority to act remained separate; authority stayed external.

The canonical numbered release series, immutable named checkpoints and recovery points, and the
separate `snapshot/*` namespace also remained governing history infrastructure. `v0.1.0` stayed
reserved for an explicit future maturity decision.

## Known limitations at the checkpoint

The implementation remained limited to `finite-linear-uncertainty.v1` and the existing decision and
compatibility operations. Revisions were explicit supplied alternatives, not inferred chronology or
automatic source-currency selection. Complete lineage enumeration was bounded and could grow on
larger dependency graphs.

Declarations were not authenticated review or empirical truth. The lifecycle supplied no automatic
belief revision, general service or database, implicit analysis-output composition, causal,
sequential, strategic, or safety semantics, or authority to act. The pinned checker, CPython, SciPy,
the standard library, and the operating system remained part of the trusted computing boundary.

## What changed next

PR [#50](https://github.com/saykig/Writ/pull/50) later added specialist repository skills for
decision integration and release history. That repository-intelligence support was not present at
the `v0.0.8` target and is not part of this historical checkpoint. No later capability is implied by
this release.

## Current retrospective

`v0.0.7` proved one bounded exact decision-case integration. `v0.0.8` established a bounded way to
preserve and reassess multiple separately authored analyses under explicit revisions while retaining
exact mathematical, provenance, applicability, and human-authority boundaries.

This was evidence that the decision layer could support a reusable revision lifecycle. It did not
solve general cumulative decision intelligence or establish the maturity reserved for `v0.1.0`.
