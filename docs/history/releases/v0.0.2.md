## What Writ was trying to do

Writ reset around a source-grounded knowledge system for political science and global affairs. The
goal was to preserve evidence, typed claims, review, uncertainty, identity, and supersession before
building downstream comparisons or applications.

## What existed at this checkpoint

The repository had explicit schema and protocol authority, an internal support layout, a shared
record grammar, legal-policy and institutional corpus families, stable corpus identities, evidence
reconciliation, and reviewed NIST Stage A records. Older G7/G20 and EU–US work remained available as
compatibility or archived evidence rather than silently defining the new architecture.

## What worked

The reset preserved evidence while changing governing abstractions. Shared source/evidence fields
could be composed with family-specific semantics without forcing one universal record type. Stable
IDs, explicit unresolved values, directional supersession, and deterministic generation survived
the migration. NIST Stage A demonstrated bounded human review over native institutional records.

## What did not survive

The universal claim that questions must always remain outside Writ was later narrowed when a
separate derived-decision layer required explicit questions. Compliance consumers, active G7/G20
repository bodies, broad constitutional-import ambitions, and corpus expansion as the main roadmap
driver were later retired. The in-repository web product still existed and was removed in the next
period.

## What survived

Schema/protocol authority, stable corpus identity, family-owned semantics, the shared provenance
envelope, evidence-state distinctions, immutable review history, and separation of native knowledge
from downstream presentation remain current.

## Known limitations at the checkpoint

NIST review was still only Stage A. Cross-family semantic interoperability, a repository-wide
verification harness, and a portable data bundle did not yet exist. The constitutional importer was
an experiment with only three committed draft records from a much larger source subset. The reset
had clarified records more than it had proved a complete product.

## What changed next

Writ expanded reviewed NIST and European Commission institutional knowledge, tested cross-family
links, built deterministic verification/export surfaces, and experimented with onboarding. The web
application then split from the repository.

## Current retrospective

This checkpoint established the durable source-grounded half of current Writ. Its strongest result
was the boundary between a shared provenance grammar and family-specific meaning, rather than the
number of corpora it could accumulate.
