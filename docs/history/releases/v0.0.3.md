## What Writ was trying to do

Writ tried to prove that reviewed institutional knowledge could remain atomic, source-grounded,
interoperable across corpus families, independently verified, and exported without inheriting a web
presentation's choices.

## What existed at this checkpoint

NIST and European Commission Stage B records, human judgments, directed supersession, and reviewed
cross-family links were active. The Writ Verification Harness checked ontology, interoperability,
provenance, and integrity through deterministic adapters. A deterministic data bundle exported the
catalogued knowledge layer. Web and onboarding experiments had also been built, then `apps/web` was
removed after the Writ-Web split.

## Major decisions

- ADR 0018 was Accepted: operational-capacity claims received bounded native semantics and evidence
  requirements.
- ADR 0019 was Accepted: cross-family relations used institution-owned Core links with strict
  approved-identity resolution.
- ADR 0020 was Accepted: deterministic verification separated orchestration, exact adapters, scoped
  rules, and human acceptance.

ADRs 0001–0017 also remained Accepted at the checkpoint.

## What worked

Human review preserved atomic institutional distinctions and explicit omissions where evidence did
not establish operational capacity. Three cross-family links were approved while 14 unresolved
candidates remained queue-only. The Verification Harness produced deterministic findings without
claiming authority to accept evidence, and the data bundle separated canonical corpus membership
from web selection.

## What did not survive

The in-repository UI, homepage, and onboarding work were removed during this period. ADR 0023 later
retired the specialized ADR-0019 verification workflow and its ADR-0020 rule pack. The period's
multi-corpus operating emphasis was later narrowed to NIST as the active evidence-grounding proving
ground.

## What survived

Human-reviewed atomic records, bounded semantic interoperability, unresolved-state preservation,
source and passage integrity, deterministic verification, exact adapter dispatch, portable bundle
semantics, and the boundary between machine findings and human acceptance remain durable.

## Known limitations at the checkpoint

The harness verified declared repository contracts rather than semantic truth. Cross-family
interoperability was limited to six named relation types and reviewed cases. The bundle was a
portable projection, not a decision system. Removing the web application left no public interface
inside this repository.

## What changed next

After a short documentation cleanup, the 22 August foundation reset made records independent of
questions and presentation, retired the remaining compliance runtime and obsolete corpus consumers,
and selected NIST as the active source-grounding reference.

## Current retrospective

This checkpoint showed that review, verification, and portable export could reinforce each other
without erasing uncertainty. Its UI work was disposable; its separation of exact mechanical checks
from human semantic acceptance became foundational.
