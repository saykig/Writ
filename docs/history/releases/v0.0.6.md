## What Writ was trying to do

Writ began moving from a portable evidence kernel toward a broader decision-provenance foundation.
The immediate work remained deliberately bounded: prove one structured-document grounding profile,
bind human-review claims to exact artifact bytes, and test consistency across parser, compiler,
schema, verifier, export, and reload boundaries.

## What existed at this checkpoint

A pure internal helper grounded two retained eCFR paragraphs from frozen XML through an explicit
selector and transformation profile. Native judgments could bind exact portable review-artifact
bytes. Bundle reload replayed more native projections, and historical judgment targets were scoped
to record migrations in the governing corpus and target kind. The temporary Track B reviewer-role
experiment and its files still existed.

## What worked

Grounding could derive the reviewed text and passage hashes from verified frozen XML without using
the expected quote as an extraction input. Review-artifact binding prevented silent substitution of
external review bytes after a manifest refresh. Cross-layer tests exposed and repaired real replay,
identity, and historical-target inconsistencies while preserving old judgments and records.

## What did not survive

The Track B reviewer-agent files and persistent role framing were retired in PR #44 after their
findings became tests, diagnostics, verification logic, and invariants. The product definition's
claim that decision provenance was only future research was superseded when PR #43 added one bounded
derived decision case.

## What survived

Bounded structured grounding, exact review-artifact association, immutable correction history,
corpus-scoped migration reasoning, cross-layer replay checks, and the distinction between byte
binding and semantic acceptance remain durable.

## Known limitations at the checkpoint

The XML grounding profile supported one document type, one selector shape, and one explicit
transformation; extracted text was parsed XML text, not a literal source-byte span. Artifact binding
proved content association, not reviewer identity, agreement, sufficiency, or truth. No executable
mathematical decision case existed yet.

## What changed next

Writ integrated one exact Decision Lab producer/checker boundary, then reset governance around two
separate surfaces. It retired the temporary reviewer roles, legacy database persistence, and
root-level historical bodies.

## Current retrospective

This checkpoint supplied the connective tissue between source provenance and decision provenance.
Its temporary agent structure was expendable; the exact bindings and consistency invariants were
the lasting result.
